\chapter{Discussion}

This chapter synthesises the findings from the evaluation, considers their
implications for educational technology practice and research, and reflects
critically on the limitations and generalisability of the work.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Interpretation of Findings}

\subsection{Usability Gains and Cognitive Load Reduction}

The primary finding of the evaluation is unambiguous: the purpose-built
platform reduced the cognitive burden of H5P interactive video authoring
dramatically compared to the WordPress-based baseline. A 65-percentage-point
improvement in task completion rate and a 93\% reduction in median task time
are effect sizes that exceed the improvement thresholds proposed by
Nielsen~\cite{nielsen1994} as indicative of a ``dramatic'' usability
difference. These improvements are consistent with the predictions of
cognitive load theory~\cite{sweller1988,chandler1991}: co-locating the
video player, timestamp capture button, question editor, and AI suggestion
panel on a single screen eliminated the split-attention effect that
characterised the WordPress workflow, in which instructors were required to
hold video timestamps and partially-composed questions in working memory
while navigating between screens.

The reduction in mean error count from 8.3 (WordPress) to 0.9 (new
platform) per participant further supports the cognitive load interpretation.
The most frequent error category on the new platform---timestamp entry in
the wrong unit---reflects a residual interface ambiguity rather than a
systemic workflow problem, and it accounts for only 6 of 9 total errors
across all 10 participants. The most frequent error on the baseline, by
contrast, was navigation-related (going to the wrong WordPress screen),
indicating that the fundamental task structure, not just individual field
interactions, was the source of difficulty.

The SUS score of 82.5 positions the platform firmly in the ``Excellent''
grade range~\cite{brooke1996}, above both the acceptable threshold of 68
and the ``good'' threshold of 80. This result, combined with the 4.8/5.0
satisfaction rating, indicates that usability improvements were perceived
subjectively as well as demonstrated objectively in task performance.

\subsection{AI-Assisted Authoring as a Pedagogical Tool}

The qualitative findings reveal an emergent behaviour that was not
anticipated in the initial design: several participants used the AI
suggestion panel not merely as a productivity accelerator but as a
pedagogical scaffold. The Bloom's taxonomy labels on suggestion cards
prompted five participants to reflect on the cognitive difficulty
distribution of their question sets, with two explicitly rearranging their
selections to achieve a more balanced spread across taxonomy levels. This
suggests that the AI pipeline's classification of suggestions by cognitive
level has value beyond accuracy filtering---it introduces educators without
formal instructional design training to a framework for deliberate cognitive
targeting.

This finding aligns with the vision articulated by Beetham and
Sharpe~\cite{beetham2019}: the most effective educational technology tools
do not merely automate existing practice but provide scaffolding that helps
educators develop their own pedagogical reasoning. An AI suggestion panel
that labels suggestions by Bloom's level acts as a lightweight instructional
design tutor alongside its role as a content generator.

The observation that 7 of 10 participants treated the AI suggestion panel
as their primary workflow anchor---starting with AI suggestions and
supplementing them with manually authored questions, rather than the
reverse---suggests a shift in mental model from ``I author questions and
the AI helps'' to ``the AI drafts questions and I curate them.'' This curation
posture places the instructor in a reviewing and judging role rather than a
generating role, which is consistent with Bloom's taxonomy's observation
that evaluation (\emph{evaluate}) is a higher-order cognitive activity than
recall (\emph{remember}) or reproduction~\cite{anderson2001}. Curation may
therefore be a more appropriate role for domain-expert educators than
question generation from scratch.

\subsection{Architectural Suitability}

The dual-provider AI architecture (Ollama first, Claude fallback) proved
pragmatically effective. Claude produced zero schema validation failures
across 20 test runs, while Ollama produced two failures that were caught by
the Zod validator and returned as structured errors. The prompt revision
that resolved the Ollama failures (adding the instruction to omit markdown
code fences) illustrates the iterative nature of prompt engineering:
structured output reliability requires prompt constraints that are specific
to each model's output tendencies. The modular AI service design, which
routes to either provider behind a common interface, allowed this fix to be
applied once without changes to the frontend or H5P integration layer.

The choice of SQLite as the database---often dismissed as unsuitable for
multi-user applications---proved well-matched to the deployment constraints.
At 50 concurrent connections, all non-AI API endpoints responded within
500~ms, satisfying NFR-01. SQLite's file-based architecture~\cite{sqlite2023}
simplifies backup (a single file copy), migration (a standard Sequelize
migration script), and local development (no database server installation
required). The schema is designed for forward-compatibility through the
Sequelize ORM~\cite{sequelize2023}: migrating to PostgreSQL would require
only a dialect change and connection string update, with no application
code modification, reflecting the database-independence architectural goal
stated in Section~4.1.

\section{Pedagogical Implications}

The results carry three practical implications for educational technology
deployment in Vietnamese universities.

\textbf{Lowering the authoring barrier expands who can create interactive
content.} The Technology Acceptance Model posits that perceived ease of use
is a necessary condition for tool adoption, particularly in contexts where
perceived usefulness is already high~\cite{davis1989}. The WordPress-based
workflow effectively excluded non-technical instructors by collapsing
perceived ease of use through its multi-screen complexity, regardless of
their instructional motivation. The new platform's 95\% task completion rate
and 3.2-minute median task time suggest that interactive video authoring can
be made accessible to the full faculty population, not just the technically
proficient minority. The multiplicative implication is significant: an
instructor who previously could not justify 45~minutes to embed a single
question can now embed three or four interactive moments within a comparable
time investment.

\textbf{AI assistance shifts effort from generation to curation.} As noted
above, the AI pipeline effectively transfers the question-generation effort
from the instructor to the model, and transfers the instructional design
effort from the model back to the instructor. Instructors spend their time
judging whether a suggested question is appropriate, accurate, and
pedagogically well-placed---tasks for which their domain expertise is
essential. This division of labour is arguably better aligned with
instructor expertise than requiring them to compose each question from
scratch.

\textbf{Bloom's taxonomy classification raises metacognitive awareness.}
Anderson and Krathwohl's revised taxonomy provides a principled hierarchy
of cognitive objectives that instructors can use to ensure their question
sets span a range of cognitive difficulty~\cite{anderson2001}. The inclusion
of taxonomy labels on AI-generated suggestion cards introduced a
metacognitive dimension to the authoring workflow that was absent in both
the baseline and the prior design. Even participants who were unfamiliar
with Bloom's revised taxonomy prior to the study were observed using the
labels to diversify their question distributions, suggesting that interface-
level exposure to a pedagogical framework can influence authoring behaviour
without requiring prior formal training in instructional design.

\section{Architectural and Engineering Implications}

The project offers several engineering lessons applicable to the broader
class of AI-augmented educational technology systems.

\textbf{Structured output validation bridges the probabilistic--deterministic
boundary.} Treating AI output as untrusted input and validating every
suggestion against a Zod schema---following the output automater pattern
described by White et al.~\cite{white2023}---prevented schema violations
from reaching the H5P content store. In an educational deployment context,
a corrupt H5P package could silently produce unrenderable or pedagogically
incorrect content in students' learning environments without triggering an
obvious system error. The validation layer absorbs the reliability gap
between probabilistic model output and the deterministic software contracts
that downstream systems depend on.

\textbf{Streaming is essential for AI-interactive UIs.} All 10 evaluation
participants preferred the SSE streaming interface over a hypothetical batch
interface. The 8--12 second generation latency for a 5-minute video is
perceptible but not disruptive when the interface provides token-level
progress feedback. Without streaming, the same latency would constitute a
blocking wait with no system status visibility, violating Nielsen's
heuristic of visibility of system status~\cite{nielsen1994}.

\textbf{Provider abstraction enables cost and privacy flexibility.}
Offering Ollama (free, locally hosted, privacy-preserving) as a first
choice with Claude as a fallback means that institutions with data
sovereignty concerns can operate the AI pipeline entirely within their
network, while still benefiting from cloud AI when the local inference
server is unavailable. This design pattern is broadly applicable to
EdTech systems in contexts where regulatory or institutional constraints
limit data transmission to external AI providers.

\section{Limitations and Boundary Conditions}

\subsection{Sample Size and Participant Pool}

The evaluation used ten participants, the minimum recommended by Nielsen
for a usability study aimed at identifying the majority of usability
problems~\cite{nielsen1994}. This sample size is adequate for qualitative
problem identification but insufficient for parametric statistical testing.
The reported means and effect sizes should be interpreted as evidence of
practically significant differences rather than as statistically controlled
point estimates. A larger study with n $\geq 30$ would be required to
compute confidence intervals and perform Mann--Whitney U tests with
adequate power.

Furthermore, the participants were IT students acting as instructor proxies.
While this is standard practice for early-stage usability evaluation, there
is a plausible ecological validity gap: actual university instructors may
exhibit different mental models, different domain expertise in their subject
matter (which affects how they evaluate AI-generated questions), and
different attitudes toward AI assistance. In particular, instructors in
non-technical disciplines may engage differently with the Bloom's taxonomy
labels than IT students who have encountered the taxonomy in their academic
training.

\subsection{Single-Session Measurement}

The evaluation measured performance in a single 30-minute session per
condition. This design captures first-use performance but does not measure
learning curves, habituation effects, or long-term adoption. A longitudinal
study tracking instructor usage over a full semester would provide evidence
on whether the initial performance advantages are maintained, whether new
usage patterns emerge (e.g., instructors developing personal AI prompt
customisations), and whether instructors recommend the platform to peers---
a strong indicator of real-world adoption potential.

\subsection{H5P Content Type Coverage}

The platform supports six of the 50+ H5P content types. While these six
types cover the most common interactive video use cases, instructors with
specific pedagogical needs (branching scenarios, drag-the-words, essay
questions) would find the platform insufficient as a sole authoring tool.
Extending the type coverage while maintaining the Zod-validated AI
generation pipeline for each new type requires both schema definition work
and prompt engineering effort proportional to the complexity of the new
type's content parameters.

\subsection{Transcript-Dependent AI Quality}

The AI suggestion quality observed in the evaluation was based on videos
with clear, English-language audio and high-quality YouTube automatic
captions. Degraded transcript quality---from noisy audio, heavy accents,
domain-specific technical vocabulary, or non-English language---would
reduce suggestion relevance. The two off-topic suggestions in the expert
quality assessment (Section~5.4) both arose from noisy transcript segments.
This transcript dependency is an inherent property of the generate-from-transcript
paradigm and cannot be fully addressed through prompt engineering alone;
it requires upstream improvements in transcription quality.

\section{Generalisability}

The design principles and architectural patterns developed in this project
are broadly generalisable beyond the HCMIU context:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The co-location design principle (reducing split-attention by
  placing all authoring tools on a single screen) applies to any
  timestamp-based content authoring tool, not only H5P.

  \item The dual-provider AI architecture (local-first, cloud-fallback)
  is applicable to any educational technology system that must balance
  AI capability with data privacy constraints.

  \item The structured output validation pattern (treating AI responses
  as untrusted input, validating against a schema, discarding non-conforming
  outputs) is applicable wherever LLMs are used to generate structured data
  for consumption by downstream systems.

  \item The SSE streaming pattern for AI progress reporting is applicable
  to any web interface where AI generation latency exceeds approximately
  3~seconds and user abandonment is a concern.
\end{itemize}

Institutions in comparable contexts---Southeast Asian universities with
similar LMS infrastructure (Moodle, Canvas), similar faculty technical
literacy profiles, and similar national digital education policy
imperatives---would likely find the platform directly deployable and the
evaluation results directionally applicable.
