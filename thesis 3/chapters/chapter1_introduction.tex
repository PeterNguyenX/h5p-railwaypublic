\section{Background}

Over the last 10 years, Vietnamese higher education has steadily moved toward digital learning. Decision No.~1373/QD-TTg~\cite{vietnam_strategy2021} calls for the use of digital technology in education at all levels, not only to equip learners with hardware equipment but also to rethink how educational content is created, shared, and used. Universities under Vietnam National University~-- Ho Chi Minh City (VNU-HCM) are expected to lead this effort.

The infrastructure is largely ready as the VNU-HCM MOOC platform~\cite{vnuhcm_mooc} (\url{https://courses.vnuhcm.edu.vn}) already provides thousands of course modules. The more challenging task is to use it effectively by faculty. Based on surveys conducted at HCMIU, 89\% of instructors would want to develop digital content that engages students, whereas only 23\% are very confident with the tools they have available. What seems to hold them back is the fact that interfaces are simply too complicated, the learning curve is too steep, and there is no meaningful help with instructional design decisions. Research on the adoption of e-learning technologies confirms that among the factors that influence tool adoption, the perceived ease of use is one of the most significant~\cite{hadullo2017}. Progress has been made in closing the infrastructure gap, but the tooling gap remains.

Among the more researched formats with regard to teaching a course asynchronously and for hybrid teaching is interactive video. The mechanism is direct: students pause and answer questions at particular points in the lecture they are watching, so that they are required to retrieve the information at the time of encoding. Such a process is well supported by the learning science literature~\cite{freeman2014}. For example, Merkt et al.\ reported that an online forced-choice quiz embedded in video resulted in higher scores in terms of retention and transfer compared with an equivalent linear video~\cite{merkt2011}, and Vural demonstrated, in online courses, that strategic placement of questions in the video led to higher scores in the post-test, long-term retention, and decreased dropout rates~\cite{vural2013}.

The design of those embedded questions matters independently. Bloom's revised taxonomy~\cite{anderson2001} gives instructors a structured vocabulary for cognitive levels, from recall through synthesis and evaluation. Questions placed at the right cognitive level for the concept just covered, and timed to appear immediately after that concept ends, reduce extraneous cognitive load while increasing germane load --- the kind of effort that produces durable understanding~\cite{sweller1988,chandler1991,paas2003}.

H5P (HTML5 Package) is the open-source framework that makes this technically possible~\cite{h5p2023,lumieducation2023}. Developed by Joubel AS starting in 2013, H5P now covers over 50 content types --- from multiple choice and true/false through branching scenarios, virtual tours, and interactive timelines. Each H5P package is a self-contained \texttt{.h5p} ZIP archive holding its own JavaScript libraries, styling, media files, and a structured JSON configuration. That portability means any H5P package can be imported into Moodle~\cite{moodle2023}, Canvas, Blackboard, or any other H5P-aware LMS using standard LTI~1.3~\cite{lti2019}.

H5P Interactive Video is the most widely used content type in higher education. It wraps a video source --- either an uploaded file or a YouTube URL --- in a player that pauses at pre-defined timestamps to show interactive overlays. Learners must respond before playback resumes. The interaction types most relevant to this project are Multiple Choice, True/False, Fill in the Blanks, Drag Text, Mark the Words, and Matching. Each is tied to a dedicated H5P JavaScript library with a strictly defined JSON schema, which matters for the AI pipeline described later.

Despite H5P's capabilities, Vietnamese universities deploy it almost exclusively through the WordPress H5P plugin --- a tool built for small personal sites, not institutional content production. Creating one interactive video requires navigating at least six distinct screens: logging into WordPress admin, finding or creating a post, activating the H5P shortcode block, opening the H5P editor in a separate iframe, uploading the video, locating each timestamp manually through the built-in player, and configuring each interaction across several nested settings screens, then saving and returning to the main post. This workflow mixes web publishing concepts into a task that has nothing to do with teaching. First-time users typically need 45--60 minutes to produce a basic three-question interactive video.

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)~\cite{davis1989} explains why this matters at scale. Perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness together account for much of the variance in faculty tool adoption. A workflow that imposes high extraneous cognitive load on every interaction --- as the WordPress H5P workflow does through its fragmented interface --- drives perceived ease of use to near zero. Adoption fails regardless of how strong the educational evidence behind the tool might be~\cite{sweller1988,chandler1991}.

Large language models have revolutionized the possibilities of automated educational content development~\cite{brown2020,anthropic2024}. An effort-saving pipeline that segments a video transcript by topic and presents structured interaction suggestions to the instructor can significantly reduce the per-question workload. The same pipeline can be used to add Bloom's taxonomy labels to each suggestion, enabling instructors to spread cognitive challenge across a video without requiring extensive training in instructional design.

The question driving this work: can a purpose-built web authoring interface, combined with such a pipeline, measurably improve educator productivity and satisfaction when creating H5P interactive videos? The primary AI provider is Groq's fast cloud API using the \texttt{llama-3.3-70b-versatile} model, with a locally hosted Ollama server available as a privacy-preserving fallback.

\section{Problem Statement}

Look at a humanities lecturer who is developing an online unit. It is a recorded lecture that she has to give a 15-minute version of and she knows she needs comprehension checks spaced throughout the lecture, but the only type of tool she can find for these is WordPress H5P: six different screens, 47 different interaction points in the interface, and almost three minutes of loading time, all while trying to keep track of a video she keeps losing her position in. After an hour she has two questions. A colleague who has more IT experience has six. The tool is not broken --- it is just not being used for what it was meant to be used.

That situation creates four reinforcing problems:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fragmentation and complexity of workflows.} The H5P workflow is WordPress-based and involves at least six different screens, which introduces concepts of web publishing that are unrelated to the teaching task. To create one interactive video, a task analysis identified 47 interactions with the interface on those screens. The average page load time for an institutional WordPress deployment is 3.2 seconds --- acceptable by itself, but 47 consecutive interactions equates to almost two and a half minutes of waiting time, with loss of video position being incurred on every interaction.

  \item \textbf{Excessive cognitive load in the system.} The effort required to operate the interface crowds out thinking about the teaching content~\cite{chandler1991,mayer2009}. When asked about time spent managing tool settings, aligning timestamps, and repositioning shortcodes, instructors reported that they were more likely to do these things than to actually compose the questions --- a direct inversion of what this type of tool is supposed to support.

  \item \textbf{Low institutional adoption.} Currently, only faculty members who are tech-savvy use H5P as part of their instruction at VNU-HCM, as the workflow requires a high level of technical knowledge. Within humanities, social science, and other non-technical disciplines --- where interactive video might benefit students the most --- instructors are most effectively cut off from a format with the best track record of evidence for positive outcomes~\cite{davis1989,hadullo2017}.

  \item \textbf{No AI assistance.} None of the freely available H5P authoring tools has a built-in feature for generating question ideas with AI. Without instructional design training, instructors have no efficient way of generating contextually relevant questions tied to what their video actually covers.
\end{enumerate}

The \textbf{primary research question} is:

\begin{quote}
  \itshape What is the potential of a web platform designed specifically for use with interactive
  video authored by university teachers to enhance the teaching and learning quality, the
  adoption of interactive video, and the efficiency of such video authoring?
\end{quote}

Four supporting questions guide the investigation:

\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.]
  \item Which usability challenges of existing H5P authoring processes most strongly inhibit users in completing tasks and are likely to affect user satisfaction?
  \item What are the user-centred design principles that can be applied to minimise the extraneous cognitive load of interactive question authoring with timestamps?
  \item What full-stack architecture best supports scalable, real-time AI-assisted question generation and H5P package assembly in a unified web environment?
  \item How much better is the new platform, quantitatively and qualitatively, on usability, efficiency, and productivity measures than the WordPress baseline?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope and Objectives}

Five concrete objectives structure the work.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Platform Development and Architecture.} Create and develop a full-stack web application that provides an interface with a visual timeline where instructors can upload or link a video file, insert H5P interactions at specific timestamps, preview the content in real time, and export a standards-compliant \texttt{.h5p} package ready to import into the VNU-HCM MOOC infrastructure and other LTI-compatible LMS environments.

  \item \textbf{AI Pipeline Integration.} Construct and integrate an AI pipeline that generates timestamped transcripts from uploaded videos (from YouTube captions, uploaded WebVTT/SRT files, or local Whisper transcription), feeds the transcript into a large language model, and outputs a validated JSON payload of H5P interaction suggestions. Each suggestion is accompanied by precise timestamps, the type of question, distractor options, and a Bloom's taxonomy classification.

  \item \textbf{User-Centred Design.} Implement user-centred design methodology in the process of designing the interface and prototyping. Every design decision is based on the identified instructors' needs and is validated against Nielsen's usability heuristics.

  \item \textbf{Security Implementation.} Apply a security baseline based on the OWASP Top Ten, including JWT-based authentication, role-based access control, server-side Zod input validation, HTTPS enforcement, and rate limiting.

  \item \textbf{Empirical Usability Evaluation.} Perform a controlled comparative usability study measuring task completion rate, time-on-task, error rate, and SUS scores against the WordPress H5P baseline. Use think-aloud protocols and post-task interviews to complement quantitative data.
\end{enumerate}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The platform's scope is bounded by the following constraints.

\textbf{Content type scope.} There are six different types of interactions supported: Multiple Choice, True/False, Fill in the Blanks, Drag Text, Mark the Words, and Matching. All six can be manually authored. All six are also generated by the AI pipeline, which is based on a pattern matrix that correlates content characteristics with question types. Each of these types has a clear JSON schema that can be validated using Zod, so that the AI output never compromises the H5P player.

\textbf{Video source scope.} The platform allows video file uploads up to 500~MB (MP4, WebM, MOV) and YouTube video URLs. Transcripts from YouTube are automatically or manually generated and retrieved using the YouTube API integration. For uploaded files, instructors can provide a WebVTT caption file or leave it to \texttt{faster-whisper}'s local invocation to complete the transcription of the audio.

\textbf{User scope.} The primary user is a university instructor or teaching assistant at VNU-HCM. A secondary Admin role handles user account management, system monitoring, and security audit log access.

\textbf{Integration scope.} The platform exports standard H5P packages compatible with any LTI-compatible LMS. LMS gradebook passback and xAPI learning analytics are designated as future work.

Known limitations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H5P content types.} Branching Scenarios, Course Presentations, and Virtual Tours are too complex to be included.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation sample.} Ten senior IT students were used as educator proxies for usability testing. This is common practice in early-stage formative evaluation~\cite{nielsen1994}, but it limits generalizability to less technically oriented demographics.
  \item \textbf{Mobile authoring.} The platform is aimed at the desktop and large tablet user base. Technically, mobile authoring is possible, but it was not a primary design goal and has not been formally evaluated.
  \item \textbf{Concurrent load.} The system was stress-tested with up to 50 concurrent users. A more sophisticated architecture, probably based on Kubernetes, would be needed for large-scale institutional deployment.
  \item \textbf{AI transcription quality.} The accuracy of the transcript will affect the quality of the AI-generated suggestions. If the audio is of poor quality or if there are strong accents, Whisper output may be compromised and the AI suggestions will be affected downstream as well.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Contributions}

The work produces five contributions to educational technology research and software engineering practice.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A reproducible platform architecture.} The thesis documents a full-stack architecture combining React~18, Zustand, Express.js, SQLite/Sequelize, the Lumieducation H5P server library, and a multi-provider AI pipeline. Documentation is detailed enough for other universities to reproduce, modify, and deploy independently.
  \item \textbf{A novel AI-assisted authoring workflow.} The pipeline that takes a raw video transcript through structured LLM prompting and returns a Bloom's taxonomy-labelled list of H5P interactions represents a meaningful integration of generative AI into the H5P ecosystem. The two-step prompting approach --- topic segmentation first, then question generation per topic using a content-type pattern matrix --- is designed to reduce hallucination and produce structured output.
  \item \textbf{Prompt engineering for structured H5P JSON output.} The system prompts, few-shot examples, and Zod validation strategies developed here reliably coerce an LLM into returning complex JSON payloads conforming to H5P content type specifications --- a reusable design pattern for structured AI output in other domains.
  \item \textbf{Empirical usability evidence.} Comparative evaluation against the WordPress H5P baseline provides quantitative evidence that a purpose-built, user-centred platform can substantially reduce authoring time, improve task completion rates, and raise SUS scores.
  \item \textbf{Open-source contribution.} The full codebase --- including AI service routing, prompt templates, database schemas, and H5P integration middleware --- is publicly available to support further research and free institutional deployment.
\end{enumerate}

Practically, the platform's impact runs in three directions. It lowers the technical barrier enough that a wider range of VNU-HCM faculty can independently produce interactive content. It shows that the gap between generative AI research and reliable educational tooling can be closed with careful software engineering. And it contributes to Vietnam's national digital transformation goals~\cite{vietnam_strategy2021} by offering a locally deployable, open-source solution that reduces dependence on proprietary software.

\section{Structure of Thesis}

\textbf{Chapter~2 -- Literature Review} covers the theoretical and technical foundations. It covers aspects of e-learning in Vietnam, the H5P architecture, practical studies on interactive video pedagogy, cognitive load theory, user-centred design, the development of large language models, the role of prompt engineering, and integration standards for LMSs. The chapter concludes with a critical review of related systems and identification of the specific research gaps this project fills.

\textbf{Chapter~3 -- Methodology} describes the research design, requirements analysis, the user-centred design methodology, the agile development approach, and the empirical evaluation protocol. It also outlines the AI pipeline architecture, including the prompt engineering strategy and pattern matrix for question type selection.

\textbf{Chapter~4 -- Implementation and Results} details the complete system implementation and the technical results that were measured, including backend and frontend architecture, H5P package assembly, security controls, and performance measurements such as AI latency and API load results. A scalability analysis and support for the Vietnamese language are also covered here.

\textbf{Chapter~5 -- Discussion and Evaluation} reports the results of the comparative usability study and discusses the themes that emerged from the qualitative questions posed by participants, using the results of the task completion rate, time-on-task, and SUS scores.

\textbf{Chapter~6 -- Conclusion and Future Work} summarizes the findings, reviews contributions against the research questions, discusses limitations, and proposes a plan for future development.
